Where it drives

Any road posted 35 mph or less.

That's most of East Texas. Neighborhood streets in Livingston, Onalaska, Coldspring, Huntsville, Lufkin, Woodville — all green light. You can cross a higher-speed road at an intersection (e.g., crossing US-59 at a light), you just can't drive along one.

Off-road and on private property, the speed limit and licensing rules don't apply at all. That's where the Terrain 6 really earns its name.

  • Residential streets — 25-30 mph speed limits, all legal
  • Most downtown streets in small East Texas towns
  • Lake-house neighborhoods on Lake Livingston, Sam Rayburn, B.A. Steinhagen
  • Cross-traffic on higher-speed roads at intersections — legal
  • Not legal: US-59, I-45, SH-19 — anywhere posted 40 mph or higher

After the install

Registration, license, insurance.

Three things to handle after we hand you the kit and the title. We walk you through all three.

Title & registration

Walk into the Polk County tax office with the title we provide. Registration is ~$50/year. They issue a license plate; we mount it.

Driver's license

Same Class C license you use for a passenger car. No special LSV endorsement needed. Passengers don't need a license.

Liability insurance

Any standard auto insurer adds an LSV to your policy for $10-20/month. Bring proof to the tax office.

Common questions

Texas LSV questions.

Are golf carts street legal in Texas?

Only in specific places. Under Texas Transportation Code §551.403, a golf cart may use public roads inside a master planned community with uniform restrictive covenants, on roads posted 35 mph or less within 5 miles of a golf course during daytime, or on streets a city has specifically authorized. In our East Texas service area no city has passed a cart ordinance, so the practical path is a cart-friendly subdivision like Cape Royale, Elkins Lake, or Waterwood. For full details see our county-by-county street-legal guide.

Can I turn a golf cart into an LSV by adding equipment?

No. A Low Speed Vehicle (LSV) is a federal classification under FMVSS 500 — it gets a 17-digit VIN at the factory, is titled and plated like a car, and may use roads up to 45 mph. Adding lights and mirrors to a golf cart does not make it an LSV. For full street access on 35-45 mph roads you need a factory-manufactured LSV; we can order one if that's the goal.

What's included in the street-equipment package?

The §551.402 statutory list: headlamps, taillamps, reflectors, parking brake, mirrors, and a slow-moving-vehicle emblem. We install, inspect, and provide the paperwork to get your Texas Golf Cart plate from TxDMV. The plate is a one-time non-expiring plate — statutory cap $10, counties charge about $15 with processing.

Do I need insurance and a driver's license?

Driver's license: yes, under AG Opinion KP-0364 (2021), any public-road use requires a valid Texas driver's license. Insurance: required when operating under a city ordinance (none of our local cities have one); not strictly required for the master-planned-community or golf-course exceptions, but strongly recommended. Most auto insurers will add a cart to your policy for $10-20/month.

Do I need to plate the cart?

Yes if you use it on any public road under one of the §551.403 exceptions — a Texas Golf Cart plate from TxDMV via Form 130-U at your county tax office. The exception: carts used only inside a master planned community (like Cape Royale) don't need to be plated.

Can kids drive a cart?

Only with a valid driver's license — same as a car — if the cart is on a public road. On private roads inside a master planned community the POA sets the rules, and many allow under-16 drivers on internal streets. On your own private property, no license needed.
