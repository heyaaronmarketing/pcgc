Are golf carts street legal in Texas?

Short answer: only in specific places. Under Texas Transportation Code §551.403, a golf cart can use public roads in four narrow situations. We'll walk through each — plus the county-by-county reality for East Texas.

The quick answer

A golf cart in Texas may legally use a public road only when one of these four things is true:

  • You're inside a master planned community with uniform restrictive covenants (Cape Royale, Elkins Lake, Waterwood).
  • You're on a beach open to vehicles.
  • You're on a road posted 35 mph or less, during daytime, within 5 miles of where the cart is parked, going to or from a golf course.
  • You're on a street a city has authorized by ordinance.

If none of those apply, the cart isn't street legal on that road. In our 4-county service area (Polk, San Jacinto, Walker, Liberty) no city has passed a golf-cart ordinance, so public-road use rests almost entirely on the master-planned-community exception.

Golf cart vs. LSV — the critical distinction

Most of the confusion about "street legal" comes from mixing up two different vehicle classes. Here's how Texas and federal law separate them:

Golf cart

  • Governed by Tex. Transp. Code §551.401-404
  • Speed-limited by design (typically 20-25 mph)
  • Public-road use restricted to the four exceptions above
  • Needs a one-time TxDMV Golf Cart plate (except in MPCs)
  • Must have headlamps, taillamps, reflectors, parking brake, mirrors, SMV emblem

LSV (Low Speed Vehicle)

  • Federal classification under FMVSS 500
  • 17-digit VIN from manufacturer
  • Titled and registered like a car; standard plates
  • Allowed on roads posted up to 45 mph
  • Liability insurance required at all times

Important: You cannot turn a golf cart into an LSV by adding equipment. The LSV classification comes from the manufacturer at build time with a FMVSS 500 VIN. A modified golf cart with lights and mirrors is still a golf cart under Texas law, bound by the same §551.403 restrictions.

County authority

None of our four counties — Polk, San Jacinto, Walker, or Liberty — has authority to authorize golf carts on county roads. Under §551.404(c), only Red River County or Gulf-coast counties can do that. The 2025 Texas Legislature considered miniature-vehicle legislation (SB 1816) but explicitly excluded golf carts from the change.

So the practical path for East Texas cart owners who want "street use" is:

  • Live in or ride into a master planned community (Cape Royale, Elkins Lake, Waterwood)
  • Use the golf-course exception if your lot is within 5 miles of Livingston Municipal, Point Blank, or another course
  • Buy a factory LSV if you want full road access on 35-45 mph streets

Common questions

The things buyers actually ask us at the shop.

Are golf carts street legal in Texas?

Only in specific places. Under Texas Transportation Code §551.403, a golf cart may use public roads in just four situations: (1) inside a platted master planned community with uniform restrictive covenants (think Cape Royale, Elkins Lake); (2) on beaches open to vehicles; (3) on roads posted 35 mph or less, during daytime, within 5 miles of where the cart is parked, going to or from a golf course; (4) on streets a city has specifically authorized by ordinance. If none of those apply, the cart is not street legal on that road.

What's the difference between a golf cart and an LSV?

They're two different vehicle classes under federal and Texas law. A golf cart is defined as a vehicle designed to carry a golfer and golf equipment, speed-limited, and governed by §551.401-404. A Low Speed Vehicle (LSV) is a federal class under FMVSS 500 — it has a 17-digit VIN, is titled and registered like a car, carries standard license plates, requires liability insurance, and may use roads posted up to 45 mph. A modified golf cart never becomes an LSV — the LSV classification comes from the manufacturer at build time.

Can I add a street-legal kit to make my golf cart an LSV?

No. Adding headlights, mirrors, and a windshield to a golf cart does not turn it into an LSV. It's still a golf cart under Texas law, bound by the same §551.403 restrictions. If you want LSV road access, you have to buy a factory LSV with the FMVSS 500 VIN.

Does my city allow golf carts on public roads?

We checked every city in our service area and none of them — Livingston, Onalaska, Coldspring, Shepherd, Point Blank, Cleveland, Liberty, Dayton, or Huntsville — has passed a golf-cart street ordinance. Huntsville voted one down 7-2 in 2020. That means in our region, public-road use rests entirely on the master-planned-community exception, the golf-course exception, or private POA roads.

Can my county authorize golf carts on county roads?

No. Under §551.404(c), only Red River County or Gulf-coast counties may authorize county-road use. Polk, San Jacinto, Walker, and Liberty counties do not have this authority — and the 2025 Texas Legislature did not change it.

What equipment is required?

A street-eligible golf cart needs: headlamps, taillamps, reflectors, parking brake, mirrors, and a slow-moving-vehicle emblem. That's the full statutory list from §551.402. It does not need turn signals, a windshield, or a horn — though those are good ideas.

Do I need a driver's license to drive one on a road?

Yes. Attorney General Opinion KP-0364 (2021) is clear: operating a golf cart on any public road requires a valid Texas driver's license. The one exception is private roads inside a master planned community where the POA sets its own rules — many allow underage drivers on their internal streets.

Do I need insurance?

Liability insurance is required when operating under a city ordinance (none of our local cities have one). It is not strictly required for the master-planned-community or golf-course exceptions — but we strongly recommend liability coverage on any cart that's driven anywhere other than your own property. Most standard auto insurers will add a cart to your policy for $10-20/month.

Do I need a license plate?

Yes — a Texas Golf Cart plate from TxDMV. It's a one-time, non-expiring plate (statutory cap $10; counties charge about $15 with processing). Fill out Form 130-U at your county tax office. The one exception: carts used only inside a master planned community don't need to be plated.

What about Cape Royale, Elkins Lake, or Waterwood — can I drive my cart there?

Yes. All three qualify as master planned communities with POA-maintained private roads under the §551.403 exception. Each has its own POA rules about equipment and speed, but Texas statute itself allows cart use on those internal streets. That's why these subdivisions have some of the highest cart ownership densities in East Texas.
